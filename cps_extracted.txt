StoreConnect – Omnichannel Retail Platform Capstone Project (CPS) 
Progressive Full-Stack Engineering Problem Statement 
CPS Delivery Model: The CPS is developed progressively during the 20-day learning phase and demonstrated on Day 21. The objective is a 
focused, working full-stack MVP—not a production-ready retail platform. 
Scope Boundary: Docker, Kubernetes and AWS are part of the learning TOC but are explicitly outside the CPS implementation scope. Python is 
also outside the StoreConnect CPS scope because it is taught after the CPS demo but CPS will be refactored in Python from Day 22 – Day 25. 
1. CPS Overview 
Project Name: StoreConnect – Omnichannel Retail Platform 
Industry: Retail / Omnichannel Commerce 
StoreConnect is a digital retail platform for a growing retailer that operates physical stores alongside an online shopping channel. The 
platform connects the customer shopping experience with store-level product availability, inventory operations and order fulfillment. 
CPS Design Principle 
Associates will evolve the same product progressively as they learn each technology. The CPS is not a separate end-of-training 
assignment; every learning module should result in a tangible improvement to StoreConnect. 
Physical-store simulation 
Physical stores are represented digitally as store/location records and inventory in the application. Associates are not expected to 
integrate with real stores, POS hardware, barcode scanners, payment systems or logistics providers. 
2. Business Problem Statement 
A growing retail organization currently manages product catalog, store inventory and customer orders through multiple disconnected 
processes. Customers may see a product as available online even when the nearest store does not have sufficient stock. Store teams have 
limited visibility into incoming online orders, inventory commitments and fulfillment status. 
The organization wants to build StoreConnect, a unified digital retail platform that enables customers to discover products, view 
availability, place orders and track fulfillment, while giving retail administrators and store operators better visibility and control over 
catalog, inventory and orders. 
Business Challenge 
StoreConnect Response 
Disconnected product and inventory information 
Provide a consistent product and availability view. 
Unclear store-level stock availability 
Maintain inventory by store/location and expose relevant availability. 
Manual order handling 
Create a digital order lifecycle with clear status transitions. 
Limited store-team visibility 
Provide operational views for inventory and order fulfillment. 
Limited event visibility 
Use a selected asynchronous event for an order/inventory business event.

 
Expose the current order status to the customer. 
 
Scope control: The above represents the target MVP. Associates should prioritize Must-Have stories first. Low-stock reporting, advanced admin 
capabilities and additional integrations are optional only if the core end-to-end flow is stable. 
6. Key Business Rules 
Rule 
Expected Behaviour 
Inventory validation 
An order should not be confirmed when required stock is unavailable. 
Stock reservation 
Once an order is confirmed, the required inventory should be reserved/reduced according to the chosen implementation approach. 
Cancellation 
Cancelled orders should release/restore reserved stock where applicable. 
Availability 
Customer-facing availability should be derived from maintained store inventory. 
Order lifecycle 
Order status transitions should follow defined rules and should not allow invalid transitions. 
Fulfillment location 
An order should be associated with a valid store/location for fulfillment. 
Event publishing 
A selected business event should be published for an important order/inventory change.

8. Non-Functional Requirements 
 
Security: protected customer/admin functions should require authenticated access. 
 
Maintainability: apply separation of concerns, meaningful naming and reusable components. 
 
Reliability: validate inputs and handle expected business/technical exceptions gracefully. 
 
Data consistency: inventory and order updates should follow clear business rules. 
 
Observability: provide useful application logs for important order/inventory operations. 
 
Testability: critical business logic and selected API/UI journeys should be automated. 
 
Usability: customer-facing flows should provide clear navigation, validation feedback and meaningful errors. 
 
Scalability awareness: service boundaries and event-driven processing should consider future growth without requiring production-
scale infrastructure.

APIs, React/TypeScript UI, PostgreSQL persistence, appropriate service boundaries, automated tests and one selected Kafka event.
